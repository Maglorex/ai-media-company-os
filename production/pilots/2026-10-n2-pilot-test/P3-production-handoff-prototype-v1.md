## 4. VO metni — S01 v3 (birebir; üretim kopyası farkı yok)
```
The NS Savannah was the first nuclear-powered merchant ship. And at sea, she worked — nineteen voyages on regular cargo routes in her first three commercial years.

But in a 1970 report to Congress, federal auditors found she needed about two million dollars more in government support every year than comparable conventional freighters on the same route.

Not a loss. Extra support.

And the biggest piece of it wasn't on the ship at all.

It was a base in Galveston, Texas. A pier. A heavy crane. A pool for spent fuel. A servicing barge called the Atomic Servant. Even a spare reactor core.

About one point one six million dollars a year.

And for two years, the only ship it served didn't come back.

The auditors traced most of the extra cost to three lines in their report: nuclear crew training, a nuclear shore staff — and that base.

A whole support system, built for nuclear ships. And there was only one.

So why did one ship have to carry all of it?
```

## 7. Visual language

**AI-1 — boş rıhtım atmosferi (B7 zemin, B8 takvim zemini)**
```
Create an original illustration for a documentary video. It is ILLUSTRATIVE, not a historical record.
Purpose in the video: an atmospheric establishing image of an industrial nuclear-ship service base on the Gulf Coast that is waiting for a ship that does not come.
Subject: a quiet waterfront service area at dusk: a concrete pier, one large gantry crane silhouette, a low flat-deck service barge moored at the side, a plain low industrial building, wide empty water. No ship at the berth.
Period / setting: 1960s American Gulf Coast, generic and unspecified — not a depiction of any specific real facility or its real layout.
Style: muted gouache / ink illustration with visible brush texture, clearly stylized, NOT photorealistic.
Composition: wide shot, low horizon, berth slightly left of center, empty sky on the right third for later labels, aspect ratio 16:9, high resolution.
Palette: deep navy, off-white, one warm amber light source.
Must NOT include: readable text, signs, logos, flags, people, any ship at the berth, radiation symbols, photographic film grain or anything imitating an archival photograph.
Output: one image.
```
**AI-2 — Atomic Servant mavnası cutaway (B7 "Atomic Servant" anı)**
```
Create an original technical illustration for a documentary video. It is ILLUSTRATIVE, not a historical record or engineering drawing.
Purpose in the video: show, in one clear image, what a nuclear-ship servicing barge was for.
Subject: a side cutaway of a long, flat, non-self-propelled service barge (roughly 3.5 times longer than it is wide). On deck: one tall deck crane. Inside the hull, exposed by the cutaway: a deep, lined storage pool filled with water for fuel elements and control rods. Keep everything simple and generic; no invented machinery, no labels.
Style: clean blueprint / technical line illustration, white and amber lines on deep navy, flat, NOT photorealistic, no 3D render look.
Composition: barge in side view across the middle of the frame, generous empty space above and below for our own labels, aspect ratio 16:9, high resolution.
Must NOT include: readable text, numbers, names, logos, flags, people, a ship, radiation symbols, glowing effects, photographic textures.
Output: one image.
```
