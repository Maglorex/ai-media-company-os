## Answers
- **Why viewers care:** A first-of-its-kind machine that actually worked still ended up costing more than it should — not because the ship failed, but because a whole support system existed for a single unit. That "first one pays for everything" trap is relatable far beyond shipping (pioneer technologies, one-off infrastructure). Human scale exists in the record: 27 of 67 crew needed nuclear training (C-29), a ~10-person shore team (C-30), a waiting facility (C-32).
- **Central question:** "If America's nuclear cargo ship worked, why did running it cost the government about $2 million a year more than it paid to support comparable conventional ships on the same route?"
- **Real packaging promise:** The ship worked (C-14) and federal auditors measured an extra annual cost against the average subsidy for comparable conventional ships on the same route (C-04, Q-01); the video shows where it came from (C-06, C-08–C-10) and why one ship carrying it mattered (C-11, Q-02).
- **Evidence sufficiency:** Strong. Primary source GAO report B-136209 (26 June 1970), quotes verified from page images (L-005); the central numbers and the "one ship system" mechanism are in the ledger as VERIFIED FACT (C-04, C-06, C-08–C-12, Q-01–Q-03). Limits are recorded: GAO did not review program effectiveness (Q-05); "could easily service 10" is an officials' opinion (C-13, D-018 → only "several").
- **Visual opportunity:** Strong. Public-domain ship photography (V-05, V-19), GAO pages with the exact cost lines (V-01, V-02, V-12, V-13), a reactor cutaway (V-08), route data (C-44) and the Galveston facility's components (C-31, C-42) — the facility itself has no usable photo, which makes it an explanatory reconstruction opportunity rather than a dead end.
- **Differentiation:** Risk: the ship's story is known and another documentary channel has covered NS Savannah (company record: backlog FC-001 note; current view count not re-verified). Our angle is different and specific: the audit's cost anatomy and the "one ship system" — evidence-led, not a general history.
- **Feasibility:** Feasible with current tools and clean assets (asset manifest; rights per evidence ledger §C — rights-ledger rows to be completed before production, DF-6). Narration will need ElevenLabs credits (Founder approval at production time). No paid assets required.

## Verdict
- **Verdict:** PURSUE
- **Recommendation:** Pursue CASE 001 on the audit-anatomy angle: the worked-but-costly paradox, a primary source that names exactly where the money went, and a strong visual mechanism (one ship carrying a system that could serve several).
- **Biggest risk:** Differentiation against existing NS Savannah coverage and the risk that "cost accounting" reads as dry; mitigated by human scale (crew, shore staff, the waiting facility) and the visual mechanism.
- **What would change this verdict:** REVISE if demand research shows the audit angle is already the dominant framing elsewhere or the packaging hypothesis cannot be made specific; KILL if differentiation cannot be shown on screen beyond what existing videos already cover.
