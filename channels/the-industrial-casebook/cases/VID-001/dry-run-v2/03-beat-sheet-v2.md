# VID-001 — Creative Treatment / Beat Sheet v2 (dry-run)

> author: COO main session (Stage 3 dry-run, 2026-10-14) · date: 2026-10-14 · status: DRAFT · skill: `documentary-creative-treatment` · supersedes: `03-beat-sheet.md` (v1) · responds to: `06-creative-qa-v1.md`

## Central question
"If America's nuclear cargo ship worked, why did running it cost the government about $2 million a year more than it paid to support comparable conventional ships on the same route?"
(Türkçesi: Amerika'nın nükleer kargo gemisi çalıştıysa, işletmesi devlete neden aynı hatta benzer konvansiyonel gemilere ödediği destekten yılda yaklaşık 2 milyon dolar fazlaya mal oldu?)

## Structure
Paradox → investigation (dossier). Opening logic: PARADOX / QUESTION (B-01–B-02) → CASE ENTRY with real stakes (B-03; CASE LABEL only — the CASE INTRO slot stays empty until Golden Master calibration, TIC-11/TIC-12) → EVIDENCE TEASE (B-04) → INVESTIGATION (B-05 onward). From B-06 the stronger question is stated openly — "why was a whole system needed for one ship?" — so the three items become an investigation, not a list.

## Beats
### B-01 — It worked
- **Story Function:** BREAK ASSUMPTION, NEW CURIOSITY
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "A nuclear merchant ship from the 1960s — wasn't it a failure?"
- **Claim:** It operated: in its demonstration phase about 90,000 miles and, according to GAO, 55 ports; in its first three commercial years 19 voyages from U.S. Atlantic ports (10 Mediterranean, 7 Northern Europe, 2 Far East); its operators called it "the most reliable ship we have operating" (per NPS).
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-14, C-44, Q-06, V-05, V-19 (port count attributed to GAO, E-6)
- **Payoff / Next Question:** Closes "did it work?"; opens "then what is this story about?"
- **Earns screen time because:** It breaks the viewer's likely assumption, so the cost paradox lands as a surprise rather than a confirmation.

### B-02 — The audit's number
- **Story Function:** NEW CURIOSITY, BRING EVIDENCE
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "If it worked, what is the problem?"
- **Claim:** In 1970 federal auditors found it cost about $2 million a year more than the average operating subsidy paid for comparable conventional ships on the same trade route.
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-04, Q-01, V-02, V-03
- **Payoff / Next Question:** States the central question with its correct comparison base.
- **Earns screen time because:** It is the paradox the packaging sells; without it there is no question to answer.

### B-03 — Why this audit existed
- **Story Function:** NEW CURIOSITY, HUMAN DIMENSION
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "Who wanted this counted, and what depended on it?"
- **Claim:** The report gave Congress information for a decision: keep operating the ship, or lay it up as proposed in the fiscal 1971 budget.
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-23, V-14, V-03
- **Payoff / Next Question:** Gives the investigation stakes; opens "so where did the money go?"
- **Earns screen time because:** It turns a cost figure into a decision with consequences, which is the real stake of the case file, and is where the CASE LABEL appears.

### B-04 — Three lines on a page
- **Story Function:** BRING EVIDENCE, SET UP PAYOFF
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "Is it the reactor? The crew? Something else?"
- **Claim:** By the auditors' own total, about $1.9 million of the roughly $2 million came from three nuclear-specific items: crew training, specialised shore staff, and a nuclear servicing facility.
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-06, V-01
- **Payoff / Next Question:** Narrows the question to three items; opens "what are these, physically?"
- **Earns screen time because:** It is the turn from mystery to investigation and teases all three answers without spending them.

### B-05 — What these numbers leave out
- **Story Function:** NEW INFORMATION
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "Doesn't nuclear fuel cost a fortune?"
- **Claim:** These figures do not include the nuclear fuel at all — according to the National Park Service the fuel was provided to the operator free — so the $2 million is a measurement of other costs, not a fuel bill.
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-16, C-38
- **Payoff / Next Question:** Keeps the measurement honest; points the viewer back to the three itemised costs.
- **Earns screen time because:** It prevents the viewer's most likely misreading of the number while claiming nothing about fuel being cheap or expensive.

### B-06 — People who had to learn the reactor
- **Story Function:** HUMAN DIMENSION, NEW INFORMATION, NEW CURIOSITY
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "Why would one ship need so much around it?" (stated openly here)
- **Claim:** Nuclear crew training cost almost $300,000 a year; 27 of the 67 crew needed nuclear training, reactor operators spending four to five months at the academy and four to five months aboard.
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-08, C-29, V-15
- **Payoff / Next Question:** First item made human; keeps the open question "and outside the ship?"
- **Earns screen time because:** It gives the first cost a human face and makes the bigger question explicit, so the next items are answers to it rather than a list.

### B-07 — The shore team
- **Story Function:** HUMAN DIMENSION, EXPLAIN SYSTEM
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "What does a ship need on land?"
- **Claim:** A shore staff of about ten specially trained people cost about $409,000 a year; port surveys, entry and departure arrangements and annual inspections required more work and planning than a conventional ship.
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-09, C-30, V-16 (wording per E-13)
- **Payoff / Next Question:** Shows the ship's cost living partly on land; points to the largest item.
- **Earns screen time because:** It reveals that much of the cost was not in the ship at all, which is the seed of the mechanism.

### B-08 — A facility in Galveston
- **Story Function:** SHOW SCALE, EXPLAIN SYSTEM
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "What could cost over a million a year for one ship?"
- **Claim:** The nuclear servicing facility at Galveston, Texas cost about $1,162,000 a year: a service barge (Atomic Servant), a 75-ton gantry crane, and a maintenance building with a bridge crane, a water-filled spent-fuel pit and a decontamination room, beside an exclusion zone of about 25 acres; Maritime supplied the spare core.
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-10, C-31, C-42, V-13 (components per E-11; no site layout)
- **Payoff / Next Question:** Largest item explained; opens "how often did the ship use it?"
- **Earns screen time because:** It is the largest share of the answer and the strongest visual opportunity: infrastructure the viewer has never seen.

### B-09 — Two years away
- **Story Function:** BREAK ASSUMPTION, HUMAN DIMENSION
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "Surely the ship was there all the time?"
- **Claim:** The ship did not return to the facility for two years, until August 1968; the 1967 and 1968 annual inspections took place in New York and facility staff travelled there.
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-32
- **Payoff / Next Question:** A system built for continuous work waits for its one ship; opens "why did it have to be carried like that?"
- **Earns screen time because:** It turns a cost line into an image of waiting, so the mechanism is felt before it is explained.

### B-10 — A one-ship system
- **Story Function:** EXPLAIN SYSTEM, CONNECT IDEAS, SET UP PAYOFF
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "Was the facility simply too big?"
- **Claim:** The auditors called it a "one ship system": the entire cost of these programmes and facilities had to be borne by the Savannah, and as long as one vessel must absorb the full cost, they keep operation expensive; the shore staff and facility could serve several nuclear ships at lower cost per ship (training is not shared).
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-11, Q-02, Q-03, C-12, C-33, V-12
- **Payoff / Next Question:** Connects the items into one mechanism without giving the final line; sets up the answer.
- **Earns screen time because:** It is the mechanism that makes every earlier beat mean something.

### B-11 — The answer, and the decision
- **Story Function:** PAYOFF
- **Viewer Question / Curiosity State:** "So why did it cost more — and what was decided?"
- **Claim:** Most of the extra — about $1.9 million of roughly $2 million — came from nuclear support built around a single ship, not from the ship failing to work; the audit points to that burden as what kept it expensive. The report was written for Congress's choice between operating and laying up; what Congress weighed is beyond this report, and the ship was deactivated at the end of 1971 (per NPS).
- **Evidence Anchor:** C-06, C-11, C-27 (INFERENCE — "the audit points to"), C-23, C-24
- **Payoff / Next Question:** Closes the central question and returns to the stake opened in B-03; ends on the museum ship with its date.
- **Earns screen time because:** It delivers the "so that's what it was" moment the packaging promised and closes the decision the case opened with, without inventing causation.
